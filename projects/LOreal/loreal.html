L’Oréal Groupe BucharestHow do you transform a global beauty brand into a workplace that people genuinely want to experience every day?

Completion Date

4000 sqm workplace fit-out including standard furniture, custom-made furniture, flooring, branding elements, acoustic solutions, and architectural interventions delivered by Allspace Interiors / A division of Workspace Studio

Key Constraint

Translating a globally recognized brand identity into a highly functional and human-centered workplace while balancing flexibility, wellbeing, sustainability, and operational efficiency within a contemporary corporate environment.

L’Oréal Groupe’s new Bucharest office began with a broader challenge than simply delivering a contemporary workplace. The project needed to redefine how the organization’s culture, values, and identity could be expressed through space while responding to the evolving realities of hybrid work, employee wellbeing, and long-term adaptability.

The existing workplace model no longer reflected the way teams collaborated, connected, or experienced the office environment. At the same time, the project required alignment with a strong global brand identity while remaining locally grounded and operationally efficient. The challenge was not only aesthetic, but organizational: creating an environment capable of supporting different modes of work simultaneously, from focused individual tasks to spontaneous collaboration and larger social interaction.

The office also needed to communicate a sense of purpose. For a company whose identity is built around beauty, innovation, and human experience, the workplace had to move beyond functionality and become an extension of the brand itself.

Our approach focused on translating L’Oréal’s values into spatial experience. Rather than treating the office as a collection of departments and meeting rooms, the project was conceived as a connected ecosystem designed around people, movement, interaction, and wellbeing.

Through workshops, stakeholder dialogue, and collaborative planning sessions, we identified the behaviors and experiences the future workplace needed to support. This informed a flexible spatial strategy built around collaborative neighborhoods, quiet focus zones, informal lounges, hospitality-inspired social spaces, and adaptable meeting environments. The result encourages both structured collaboration and spontaneous interaction while allowing employees to choose how and where they work throughout the day.

Materiality and product specification played a central role in reinforcing the project’s identity and long-term performance. Warm wood finishes, tactile textiles, soft lighting, and subtle metallic accents were selected to create an atmosphere that feels refined yet approachable. Natural light was prioritized throughout the layout, supporting wellbeing and energy performance while enhancing the overall spatial experience.

The furniture strategy focused heavily on ergonomics, flexibility, and durability. Herman Miller ergonomic seating was selected to support long-term comfort and workplace wellbeing, while Herman Miller Nevi height-adjustable desks introduced flexibility and movement into the daily work experience. Acoustic comfort was addressed through integrated Impact Acoustic sound-absorbing solutions alongside high-performance Maars partition systems with 42 dB acoustic insulation, allowing open collaborative areas and focused workspaces to coexist efficiently within the same environment.

Flooring selection also contributed significantly to the atmosphere and functionality of the project. Mohawk carpeting, with its natural material-inspired textures and strong acoustic performance, helped soften the workplace environment while ensuring durability under intensive daily use. Social and informal collaboration areas were complemented by selected B&T seating furniture, reinforcing the hospitality-driven character of the workspace.

What differentiates the project from a standard corporate fit-out is the intentional balance between operational performance and emotional experience. Rather than maximizing density or relying on generic workplace formulas, the design prioritizes comfort, identity, and human connection. Every circulation path, visual connection, and transition between spaces was carefully considered to support a workplace that feels intuitive, inclusive, and alive.

The office ultimately became more than a functional environment. It became a physical expression of L’Oréal’s culture, one that supports creativity, collaboration, and belonging while reinforcing the company’s long-term vision for the future of work.

THE OUTCOME
  • Utilization / Adoption: The office has been fully operational and in active use since March 2025.
  • Timeline / Budget Performance: The project was delivered through a phased implementation process : 3 months dedicated to fit-out works, partitions, flooring, and building installations, 2 months focused on furniture integration, custom-made elements, branding, and decorative layers.
  • Client Feedback: The client expressed a high level of satisfaction with both the process and the final result. The collaboration benefited from strong involvement and support from the client team throughout all project phases, enabling efficient decision-making and alignment. Following completion, the company received a formal letter of recommendation acknowledging the quality of the outcome and the collaborative process behind it.
  • Long-Term Impact: One year after relocation, the client continues to actively use and positively evaluate the workplace environment. Despite the continuous growth of the team and multiple additions requested during the first year of operation, the original design strategy remained fully relevant, with no major interventions, replacements, or spatial reconfigurations required.
THE DIFFERENTIATION

A conventional workplace solution would have focused primarily on efficiency, density, and standardized planning strategies. Our approach began from a different premise: that the workplace is a strategic tool for culture, identity, and human experience.

Instead of designing around fixed functions, we designed around behaviors and relationships. Instead of treating aesthetics as decoration, we used design to communicate purpose and belonging. The result is a workplace that performs operationally while creating a deeper emotional connection between people and the environment around them.

For L’Oréal Groupe, the Bucharest office is not simply a new headquarters. It is a workplace designed to embody the values of the brand itself.
