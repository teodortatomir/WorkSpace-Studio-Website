Workplace Strategy

Workplace research. Applied to your organisation.

Space analysis, organisational insight and stakeholder priorities translated into a clear brief for design and engineering. Grounded in MillerKnoll research and global workplace evidence, then calibrated to your organisation.

Before design begins, the role of the workplace within the organisation must be clearly defined.

We analyse how people work, how teams connect, which activities benefit from physical presence and where the existing environment creates friction. Through space analysis, organisational mapping and stakeholder alignment, we translate business priorities into a clear, actionable brief for design and engineering.

The result is a framework that defines the right balance of settings for focused work, collaboration, learning and social connection; aligns space with the organisation’s culture and operating model; and establishes measurable priorities for utilisation, employee experience and workplace performance.

Our approach combines MillerKnoll research, global workplace intelligence, post-occupancy evidence and the insight gained from delivering over 500,000 m² of corporate interiors across Romania. This gives our clients more than a design brief: it provides a defensible basis for investment, grounded in how their organisation works today and how it needs to evolve.

Available as a standalone advisory service or as the first phase of our integrated Design & Build process, the service ensures that every decision serves a defined purpose—and every square metre earns its place.

What the strategy defines

Business priorities, translated into a workplace that performs.

Our analysis focuses on three areas that shape the brief for design and engineering.

Ways of working

We examine how work happens across the organisation: which teams need to collaborate, which roles depend on quiet and focus, and where formal and informal interaction takes place. These patterns define the right mix of individual, shared and social settings.

Future space needs

Growth plans, organisational change, hybrid attendance and seasonal variation all influence future demand. We use realistic projections to establish capacity, identify where flexibility is needed and ensure the workplace can adapt without unnecessary disruption or cost.

Workplace performance

We define what the workplace needs to deliver—from employee experience and engagement to effective space use and purposeful attendance. Clear objectives and post-occupancy measures allow performance to be assessed and the workplace to evolve as needs change.

Our process

Four phases. One clear brief for design and delivery.

1. Assessing the space

We begin by understanding how the existing workplace performs—or, for a relocation, what the new space makes possible. Walk-throughs, observation and utilisation data reveal how space is used, how people move through it and which areas are over- or underused.

2. Understanding the organisation

Working sessions with leadership, HR, facilities and employees build a clear picture of business priorities, working patterns, team structures, growth plans and culture. This establishes what the workplace needs to support, both now and in the future.

3. Defining the opportunity

We compare how the organisation works today with how it expects to operate over the next three to five years. This identifies the challenges the workplace must resolve, the opportunities it can unlock and the behaviours its design should support.

4. Aligning the project brief

The findings and agreed decisions are consolidated into a written project brief, aligned across leadership, HR, facilities and procurement. It becomes the shared basis for architecture, engineering, cost planning and delivery.

Key moments

When workplace strategy creates the most value.

Before significant decisions about people, space or investment are made.

New lease, relocation or expansion

Before layouts are drawn, we define requirements for headcount, working patterns, team adjacencies, amenities and future flexibility. This ensures the space is selected and planned around how the organisation needs to operate—not simply around the available floor plate.

New ways of working

As hybrid policies and attendance patterns evolve, the workplace must evolve with them. We identify how and why people use the office, then translate those behaviours into requirements for focus, collaboration, learning and connection.

Organisational change or growth

Growth, restructuring, mergers and acquisitions or new team structures can quickly make an existing office less effective. We assess future needs, identify sources of friction and define what should be retained, adapted or redesigned.

Workplace performance concerns

When employee experience, engagement, retention or space utilisation falls short, workplace strategy helps establish the role of the physical environment. We separate assumptions from evidence and define targeted changes with outcomes that can be measured after occupation.

Investment planning and approval

When leadership or the board needs a clear basis for approving a workplace investment, we bring together space requirements, future demand and business priorities. The result is an evidence-based case for the proposed scope, budget and expected value.

Engagement models

Independent advice or integrated delivery.

Workplace Strategy is available as a standalone service or as the first phase of an integrated Design & Build project.

Standalone advisory

An independent assessment with no commitment to further design or delivery. The outcome is a clear workplace brief that can guide an internal team, appointed designer or general contractor—and provide a confident basis for going to market.

Integrated Design & Build

Workplace Strategy becomes the first phase of the project, with findings carried directly into design, engineering, cost planning and delivery. One team maintains continuity from the initial brief through execution, reducing the gap between strategy and what is ultimately built.
